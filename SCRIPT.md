# English Voice-over Script — v1.0

**Reading style:** clear, documentary, conversational; not panicked. **Narrative timing:** scene windows below total exactly 2:00. Use natural pauses; adjust wording slightly after a voice test without altering factual meaning.

## 1. The Hook (0:00–0:12)

You're three years old. You tap a video on a parent's tablet. You can't even read yet. But your digital footprint may already be beginning.

## 2. First Videos (0:12–0:25)

In 2026, Dutch children aged zero to six spent an average of 102 minutes a day looking at screens. Video viewing can leave traces.

## 3. First Smartphone (0:25–0:40)

At nine, our character gets a first phone. Dutch research puts the average at 9.2 years. With a personal device, online activity can become more independent.

## 4. First Social Account (0:40–0:53)

At eleven comes a first social media account. In one Flemish study, the reported average was ten years and three months. Likes and follows add clues.

## 5. Online Identity (0:53–1:07)

By fourteen, videos, messages and shared posts can reveal interests and social connections. Not every action is public, and not every service collects the same information.

## 6. Digital Consumer (1:07–1:21)

At eighteen, shopping, subscriptions and everyday apps add more kinds of data. In-game purchases often begin even earlier. But this isn't one giant profile shared by everyone.

## 7. From Data to Prediction (1:21–1:39)

Here's the twist: data can become predictions. If someone watches running videos and searches for training tips, a platform might infer they're interested in running. That's a guess, not a fact.

## 8. The Feedback Loop (1:39–1:50)

That guess can influence recommendations. You watch, click, or scroll. Your response gives the system more signals. The loop keeps going.

## 9. The Open Ending (1:50–2:00)

So as these systems learn from your choices, how much might they shape what you choose next?

## Narration notes
- This is written for a brisk but understandable delivery (~115–120 words/min including transitions).
- Do not let speech run beyond the assigned segment. Optional voice-over should be local/static and optional for silent playback.
- On-screen source captions are NOT part of the spoken script.
- The narration says “at nine”, “at eleven”, etc. about *one fictional character*, not all children.
- The line about under-18 advertising constraints belongs in Sources & Methodology, not as an unsupported claim about all platforms.
