# Storyboard — nine scenes / exact 120 seconds

**Structure:** 0:00–0:12 hook; 0:12–1:21 growing up online; 1:21–1:39 data-to-inference; 1:39–1:50 feedback loop; 1:50–2:00 ending.

## Scene 1 — The Hook (0:00–0:12)

**Purpose/visual:** Fade from black to a child silhouette and an unbranded tablet. A single video card appears; one faint observation node glows. Do NOT label the child as identified by an ad network.

**Main on-screen text:** `Your digital footprint may start before you can read.`

**Observations to illustrate:** Video playback (possible)

**Possible inferences to illustrate:** none

**Source IDs:** fictional storytelling / no numeric factual claim

## Scene 2 — First Videos (0:12–0:25)

**Purpose/visual:** Animate a 102-minute counter next to a clearly labelled population chart. The number is the GROUP average, never a statistic about our specific 3-year-old. Reveal watch/search history as possible service-local signals.

**Main on-screen text:** `102 minutes/day  ·  Dutch children aged 0–6  ·  2026`

**Observations to illustrate:** Watch activity (if recorded), Search activity (if enabled)

**Possible inferences to illustrate:** none

**Source IDs:** S01, S04

## Scene 3 — First Smartphone (0:25–0:40)

**Purpose/visual:** Timeline moves to age nine. Tablet shrinks; the silhouette gains a phone. Add distinct app bubbles without brand logos. Label the phone statistic as parent-reported and not universal.

**Main on-screen text:** `9.2 years  ·  Average first phone age  ·  Netherlands`

**Observations to illustrate:** App interactions (possible), Device activity (possible)

**Possible inferences to illustrate:** none

**Source IDs:** S02

## Scene 4 — First Social Account (0:40–0:53)

**Purpose/visual:** Animate a sample account card and likes/follows floating toward the user’s platform-local data cluster. Mark age eleven as FICTIONAL; 10y3m as the study mean for the eligible respondents.

**Main on-screen text:** `10 years, 3 months  ·  Parent-reported first account  ·  Flanders`

**Observations to illustrate:** Likes (example), Follows (example)

**Possible inferences to illustrate:** none

**Source IDs:** S03

## Scene 5 — Online Identity (0:53–1:07)

**Purpose/visual:** Split screen into voluntarily shared content versus observed interactions. Use privacy lock symbols for private messages; DO NOT display private message content as always accessible to advertisers. Visual is illustrative, not proof of how a named provider processes data.

**Main on-screen text:** `What you share  ≠  What a platform observes`

**Observations to illustrate:** Interactions, Shared content (when posted)

**Possible inferences to illustrate:** none

**Source IDs:** S05

## Scene 6 — Digital Consumer (1:07–1:21)

**Purpose/visual:** Draw SEPARATE translucent containers for video, social, and shopping platforms with no default cross-platform arrows. Mention earlier game purchases as a caption; do not imply age 18 marks the first online purchase.

**Main on-screen text:** `More services. More types of data. Not one universal database.`

**Observations to illustrate:** Shopping activity (example), Subscriptions (example)

**Possible inferences to illustrate:** none

**Source IDs:** S06

## Scene 7 — From Data to Prediction (1:21–1:39)

**Purpose/visual:** Zoom into ONE hypothetical service. Three observed running-related actions converge toward a purple predicted-interest chip. Purple inference must be labelled “possible” and must not count as a proven personal fact.

**Main on-screen text:** `Observed signals → Possible inference: Interested in running`

**Observations to illustrate:** Running videos (fictional), Training searches (fictional)

**Possible inferences to illustrate:** Possible interest: Running (illustrative)

**Source IDs:** S05, S07

## Scene 8 — The Feedback Loop (1:39–1:50)

**Purpose/visual:** Show one loop: purple predicted interest → amber recommendation → cyan interaction → return to model. Never portray an algorithm as always perfectly predicting or manipulating actions.

**Main on-screen text:** `Predict → Recommend → Interact → Learn`

**Observations to illustrate:** Click / skip (illustrative)

**Possible inferences to illustrate:** Recommendation signal (illustrative)

**Source IDs:** S04, S05

## Scene 9 — The Open Ending (1:50–2:00)

**Purpose/visual:** Fade the previous elements. Pause on the fictional profile silhouette and the open question. End with small “Research sources & methods” cue. Avoid dystopian certainty.

**Main on-screen text:** `How much might they shape your next choice?`

**Observations to illustrate:** none

**Possible inferences to illustrate:** none

**Source IDs:** fictional storytelling / no numeric factual claim

## Transitions
- The same central person/avatar persists (or reappears at consistent position) across age-milestone scenes.
- Persistent timeline along bottom with selected markers for ages 3 / 9 / 11 / 14 / 18.
- The number of visible *illustrative category chips* may increase, but never present this as an evidence-based quantity.
- At 1:21 transition to focus on ONE service-local example (running).
- At 1:39 transform the example into a closed loop.
- 1:50 camera 'pulls back' / visual simplifies for one concise final question.

## Methods panel
- Opening note: "This is a fictional life story using research about different age groups. Visual data traces are illustrative, not your real data."
- Link each numerical claim to its source ID and make source available from the app.
- State distinct country/population of sources (Netherlands vs Flanders).
- Explain why service clusters are separate and how inference is uncertain.
- Show the minor-targeting DSA caveat.
